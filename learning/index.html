EVIDENCE-INFORMED TEACHING DESIGN

Built around a lesson.
Not a buzzword.

The current learning prototype maps to Pearson HND Business Unit 20, Organisational Behaviour. The learning sequence follows a case-based pattern: situation → limited evidence → team decision → visible consequence → facilitated reflection.

LO1 — Personality & perception

Recognise different viewpoints and reflect on how assumptions influence management decisions. A reflection prompt—not a psychometric diagnosis.

LO2 — Motivation

Compare financial incentives, autonomy, voice and fairness in a fictional staff rota. Explain the theory used rather than choosing a textbook answer.

LO3 — Team activity

Make and defend group choices, negotiate conflicting priorities and explain how teamwork influenced the eventual response.

LO4 — Power, politics & culture

Distinguish formal sponsor rights from informal influence, employee voice and organisational norms.

Connected business subjects

Marketing, customer experience, operations, negotiation, financial trade-offs and strategy appear naturally inside the same case.

Instructor-led reflection

Each scene ends with a reasoned discussion question. A team score is not evidence of an individual's leadership capability or learning gain.

References: Pearson Higher Nationals in Business, Harvard Business School case-method guidance, CIPD high-performing teams evidence review. We use our own story and teaching materials; no affiliation, approval or formal validation is claimed.

COMMON QUESTIONS

What is available now?

Can I start playing without booking or entering a room code?

Yes. The experimental Opening Night preview is a standalone, single-device browser game. Choose two to four fictional teams and begin. Each team takes a turn on the same screen.

Does it currently support 25 people on individual phones?

Not in this learning prototype. The older Business Crisis staging implementation has separate server-backed multiplayer infrastructure with an unvalidated 30-person software ceiling. The new teaching case has not yet been integrated into that server-backed room engine.

Is the game validated for university assessment?

No. It is mapped conceptually to learning outcomes from a published Pearson specification and informed by case-based teaching principles. Each institution and lecturer must decide how it fits their syllabus and assessment rules.

Can an agency or trainer buy a licence?

Licensing, booking, pricing, support and service-level commitments have not launched. The first priority is verified learning experience and reliable facilitation.

Will everyone be forced to take a leadership test?

No. Participants play as teams making decisions about a fictional event. The learning-focused prompts are optional in a social session. The game does not label people's personality types or certify their competency.
