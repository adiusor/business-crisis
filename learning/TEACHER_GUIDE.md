# OPENING NIGHT — TEACHER & FACILITATOR PACK (experimental v0.1)
**Publisher:** independent working prototype under Business in Play (provisional name).  
**Purpose:** optional experiential discussion in a class or corporate event. **Not:** graded assessment, accredited course, Harvard/Pearson licensed case, psychometric test, or validated measure of workplace competence.

## Before running the game: important boundary
- **What exists:** browser-openable, single-device interactive six-scene case for **2–4 fictional teams** taking turns on the same screen. No login or Cloudflare room-code needed. It uses the same extracted deterministic economic/risk engine as the original Business Crisis.
- **Not yet delivered:** real separate-device multiplayer integration for this scenario, instructor dashboard, an LMS export, event-booking system, payment or licencing. Do not invite 25 people to connect with their phones to this experiment.
- The model uses invented cash costs and risk/trust indices to make trade-offs visible; it is **not a validated business forecast**.
- The current prototype does not automatically establish that a student met a Pearson assessment criterion. A lecturer must set separate evidence tasks and assess them independently.

## Exact primary curriculum reference
**Pearson BTEC Higher Nationals in Business, specification Issue 5 (May 2025), Unit 20 Organisational Behaviour Management**, Level 5, 15 credits, unit code R/650/2920.

Four outcomes (short descriptions, paraphrased):
- **LO1:** Reflect on one's own personality, perception and individual differences in managing others.
- **LO2:** Apply both content and process motivation theories to a workforce.
- **LO3:** Lead a group activity and demonstrate effective team leadership.
- **LO4:** Explain how organisational power, politics and culture influence behaviour and organisational goals.

Official specification, Unit 20 starts printed page 214 and LO definitions printed page 215 (PDF pagination differs): https://qualifications.pearson.com/content/dam/pdf/BTEC-Higher-Nationals/Business/2021/hncd-l45-business-rqf-specification.pdf

## Six scenes, with explicit learning use
| Scene | Playable business situation | Required human interaction | Academic teaching hook | What *not* to claim |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 — The Invitation | 1,100 people registered for 320 places | Role debate: an early guest vs the operations limit | LO3 team communication; perceptions/assumptions (LO1 reflection) | The game itself does not prove self-awareness |
| 2 — The People | Overloaded rota, junior employee Sam suggests rotation | Group huddle; decide whether to hear and use Sam's proposal | LO2: Herzberg motivators/hygiene vs Adams fairness, role of intrinsic/extrinsic incentives; LO3 participation | Paying a bonus is not a universal cure |
| 3 — The Supplier | Shortfall of event guest packs; real second source | Negotiation and BATNA comparison; optional cash allocation in economic engine | Negotiation/practical operations, adjacent to Pearson Unit 37 and procurement; LO3 collaboration | Supplier quote is fictional, not a market price |
| 4 — The Culture | Sponsor requests control beyond the contractual scope | Decide who owns which sign-offs; test boundaries of authority | LO4: formal power, politics, culture; reference Schein's cultural levels | A single vote is not proof of a good organisational culture |
| 5 — The Partner | £120k *future annual* sponsorship with exclusivity vs £45k fallback | Negotiate concessions and walk-away point; optional cash allocation | Stakeholder negotiation, opportunity cost, LO3/LO4 | Future contract value is not money already in the bank |
| 6 — Opening Night | Credits, promises, staff fatigue and responsible expansion | Closing speech and evidence-based debrief | LO1 reflective practice, LO2–LO4 synthesis; team comparison | High score does not mean someone is a better manager |

**Concept fidelity:** the original HND specification lists theories of motivation including Herzberg, Maslow, McClelland and Alderfer (content) and Adams, Latham & Locke, Porter & Lawler (process). Use primary texts/instructor teaching notes to avoid turning theories into simplistic advice.

## Classroom lesson design

### 60-minute seminar (recommended first pilot)
0–5 min: Explain this is a fictional choice game, not a test of personality. Split the room into 2–4 teams, select Classroom mode, give each team one spokesperson per scene and rotate spokesperson after each decision.
5–10 min: Assign balanced roles so a quieter participant can offer a view; explain the **two-adviser-information limit** and cash/trust/risk axes.
10–40 min: Six scenes. Each team reads the same public evidence but selects only two confidential adviser notes. Allow ~3 min for scenario and debate, then register choices. On a single device, **pass the device privately** when reviewing adviser notes so competing teams do not see them.
40–50 min: Compare outcomes. Identify one good decision which was costly, one affordable decision which created future problems, and one genuine disagreement that improved the team result.
50–60 min: Individual reflection (not automatically collected): two assumptions I made; a colleague's perspective that changed mine; one content/process motivation theory that explains a choice; one practical team/leadership habit to improve.

Note: Two teams taking consecutive turns through six chapters with evidence, advisers, budget and debrief may exceed 60 minutes. Trial the timing with real students, then compress or allow a 90-minute seminar. **Do not pretend the time is validated.**

### 90-minute seminar
0–10 rules and learning objectives; 10–20 scene 1 evidence and roles; 20–40 scenes 2–3 on motivation/negotiation; 40–55 scene 4 power and culture; 55–70 scenes 5–6 partner and board; 70–90 structured case discussion and personal reflection.

### A 45-minute corporate team event (hypothesis)
Skip lecture terminology, use social mode and short per-scene debriefs, focus on the role conflict and competing team solutions, finish with awards for thoughtful behaviour. **A full six-scene, sequential shared-device experience may be too long; do not sell a 45-minute guaranteed duration without rehearsal.**

## Lecturer prompts / theory-to-action bridge
1. **LO1 – Perception:** Did the manager interpret Sam's quietness as lack of initiative? What other explanations exist? How might your own previous experience have changed that judgement?
2. **LO2 – Content:** Compare Herzberg's hygiene factors (e.g. working conditions) with potential motivators (responsibility, recognition). Which plan changed which? Discuss limitations.
3. **LO2 – Process:** Using Adams' equity theory, what might seven employees on double shifts perceive as unfair? How would you measure and respond?
4. **LO3 – Team:** Who actually invited different opinions? How did the team handle a tie? Which communication practice helped the group decide with incomplete evidence?
5. **LO4 – Power:** Identify legitimate sponsor rights, a manager's formal authority, and an employee's informal influence. How do you prevent slow approvals and unsafe silence?
6. **Transfer:** Is a higher cash balance always better if employee capacity and customer trust deteriorate? Provide a specific moment from the simulation.

## How to evaluate learning responsibly
- **Pre/post knowledge check:** two short scenario questions using the same rubric, administered independently by the instructor; record group sizes and conditions.
- **Observed behaviours:** record who contributed, whether evidence was cited, whether a counterargument was considered, and whether the group revised a view. Do not infer personality from these observations.
- **Reflection quality:** evidence → theory → alternative → justified recommendation, using a rubric that the instructor approves.
- **Engagement:** anonymous 1–5 enjoyment/clarity/usefulness ratings, willingness to recommend, and one free-text improvement, collected only with suitable privacy notices.
- **Usability:** setup time, spontaneous question count, episode completion, major confusion, interruption/rescue burden, and ability to explain the game unaided.
- **Commercial:** buyer interviews and paid pilot conversion only *after* product reliability and format validation. Never claim that a prototype has 'guaranteed results'.

## Moderator script (usable without slides)
**Opening (30 sec):** "You're running a fictional creative venue tonight. You have limited cash, incomplete information and an open guest list. Every team sees the public facts but must choose which two experts to consult. There is no perfect answer; explain yours."
**Before a decision:** "What do we know? What are we assuming? Who disagrees? Which cost are we willing to accept?"
**After a consequence:** "What changed in the ledger? Who gained, and who paid? Which earlier promise has returned to us?"
**Closing:** "You are not being measured as a personality or a leader. Use the story to discuss the evidence and what you would improve in a real group."
