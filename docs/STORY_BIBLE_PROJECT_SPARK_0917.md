# THE 09:17 CHALLENGE — NEW STORY BIBLE
## Scenario: PROJECT SPARK — The Opportunity
**Created:** 5 November 2026 · **State:** FOUNDER-REQUESTED FULL NARRATIVE REDESIGN, FOR REVIEW · **NO GAME-CODE CHANGE OR DEPLOYMENT**  
**Source:** independent Game Director chat; preserves one canonical staging service.

### Why this exists
The previous story remains an accidentally distributed *restructuring proposal*, 40 potential roles cut, hiring freeze, hostile press speculation and exhaustion. That is a coherent management-crisis simulation, but a poor default social occasion for mixed corporate teams and classrooms. Merely changing the title, adjectives and response labels did not answer the founder's repeated request for a **different plot**. This bible replaces the entire cause, objective, world, timeline, evidence, choices, character motivations and possible endings while retaining the valuable multiplayer **mechanics**.

### Positioning and emotional promise
**The 09:17 Challenge** — *"The message is out. The clock is ticking. What happens next is up to your team."*  
Alternative more positive teaser for A/B testing: *"One unexpected opportunity. Six decisions. Your team's next big story."*

**Genre:** A fast, uplifting, high-stakes collaborative business adventure; entertainment first, smart business trade-offs second. No fictional layoffs, victim blaming, hidden employee performance diagnosis, threatening leadership exam or real-world executive authority claims. A fun, approachable shared challenge with meaningful competing priorities and a memorable result. Appropriate for office teams, managers, apprentices, university students and mixed non-specialist groups. Same core narrative, with different optional facilitator discussion prompts.

### World and internally consistent stakes
A fictional, medium-size company operates learning and visitor experiences for businesses, schools and communities. It employs around 600 staff **across its entire business**, not on this one contract. The player teams are cross-functional response groups presenting competing plans to the company board.

At **09:17**, an existing national client sends an unexpected invitation: the company is a finalist to develop **Project Spark**, a **90-day pilot** turning under-used public spaces into surprising, accessible pop-up experiences for colleagues, families and students. The client wants a credible, affordable, inclusive proposal **by 17:30 today** because its funding committee meets that evening. The plan — not the complete installation — is due today. An existing **£240,000/year service contract** is also approaching renewal; that revenue is never today's available cash. The company has a separate **£260,000 organisation-wide 90-day improvement/contingency reserve** that protects existing services as well as financing preparatory decisions. It is not a free £260k pot solely for winning a £240k contract. The potential new pilot/expansion has **no guaranteed revenue** in the ledger.

The narrative's central tension is not "who leaked the memo?" but **"How ambitious can we be without promising what people, suppliers or venues cannot deliver?"** Every chapter forces teams to choose what to protect and what to trade away. A partner's scheduled public teaser in the afternoon raises expectations, but the fictional firm is **not yet contracted** for the new project. The final verdict must describe **plan readiness**, not pretend a client signed a deal.

**Story structure:** six chapters on the same business day (09:17, 10:06, 11:24, 13:12, 15:40, 17:30), then a 90-day forward-looking projection only in the debrief. Nothing that normally takes weeks can magically be completed in eight hours. A same-day prototype dry run is possible because a basic demonstration already exists.

### What players understand in 45 seconds
> **09:17.** Your company has a shot at something big: turning ordinary city spaces into experiences people will actually want to visit. The client wants a workable pilot plan before its board meets tonight. Your teams have one day, a shared business reserve and six decisions. Make a promise, test it against reality and see what you can deliver. The clever choice is not always the expensive one.

**Player wins emotionally** by earning a well-argued, fair, visible outcome together. **Game awards** can honour different strengths (most trusted, cash protected, lowest risk, clear communication). Scoring is a simulated model, not a leadership certification.

## Six-scene production narrative — written for English-language gameplay
Every chapter contains: a short dramatic hook; one plain-English dilemma; **three distinct, defensible plans**; **three brief public communications**; **three source exhibits**; a visible consequence; a cliffhanger tied to the next scene. Costs below reflect existing spend magnitudes **as starting design placeholders**, not yet validated against new risk deltas. No existing numeric scoring coefficients may be pasted over without recalibration, modelling, privacy checks and founder sign-off.

### 01 / 09:17 — THE INVITATION
**Hook:** "A message marked HIGH PRIORITY lands at 09:17. Your most important partner has chosen your company as one of two finalists for Project Spark. They want a pilot across up to three locations. Your initial approach is due before lunch; the complete board proposal is due at 17:30. Everyone has a different idea of what 'ready' means."
**Question:** **What are you prepared to promise before you've checked the details?**
**Exhibits:**
1. **THE INVITATION / 09:17**: "We would like a credible 90-day pilot proposal. Show delivery options for one to three locations. No award or rollout is approved."
2. **THE DRAFT CONCEPT / 09:10**: sketches for three playful, accessible learning spaces; the mock-up is **not** an approved specification.
3. **FINANCE NOTE / 09:24**: £260k organisation-wide 90-day reserve; £240k/year existing client contract under review; future income cannot fund today's promise.
**Plans (initial spend hypotheses):**
- **DESIGN THE PROOF (£18k):** verify feasibility, accessibility and costs before offering a phased proposal. Strong credibility/control; slower creative momentum.
- **MAKE THE BIG BID (£32k):** develop a compelling three-location blueprint and reserve specialist capacity immediately. Strong client excitement; higher cost, schedule and staff exposure.
- **START SMALL, SCALE SMART (£9k):** promise one strong proof-of-concept with clearly priced expansion gates. More cash protected; reduced immediate headline appeal.
**Public messages:** "We'll show you a tested plan" / "We're exploring the full three-site ambition" / "We'll prove the first site before making a bigger promise."
**Consequence:** client response recognises clarity/ambition/focus according to the plan; next chapter confronts the availability of people who must deliver it.
**Never:** call the pilot won or treat the contract value as available cash.

### 02 / 10:06 — THE PEOPLE BEHIND THE PLAN
**Hook:** "The proposal looks brilliant on the wall. The delivery team has one question: who is actually building it? One site leader wants an open workshop; operations wants named owners today; a specialist says they can help, but not for free."
**Question:** **How do you create momentum without spending your people's energy twice?**
**Exhibits:**
1. **CAPACITY BOARD / 09:56**: confirmed capacity and non-negotiable current client commitments, showing one team already fully booked.
2. **THE STAFF IDEA / 10:03**: educators and front-of-house staff identify a simple inclusion feature absent from the original concept.
3. **THE SUPPORT OFFER / 10:04**: a qualified external crew can support design/delivery with capped terms and a future handover cost.
**Plans:**
- **CO-DESIGN THE EXPERIENCE (£16k):** protected staff workshop and cross-team input. Strong ownership, inclusion and creative quality; management time moves off delivery.
- **SET THE OWNERS (£8k):** appoint accountable leads, prioritise existing tasks and get a reliable plan moving. Fast and cash-efficient; weaker employee voice if used alone.
- **BUY BREATHING ROOM (£29k):** add skilled temporary support and protect existing teams. Better capacity and wellbeing; upfront/future expenditure and onboarding risk.
**Public messages:** "The people delivering this will shape it" / "We have accountable owners and deadlines" / "We're adding support so current work remains protected."
**Consequence:** staffing capacity and the inclusion feature affect the supplier evaluation. Do not imply jobs were cut or staff were forced into unsafe shifts.

### 03 / 11:24 — THE PARTNER DEAL
**Hook:** "Three suppliers can power the experience. One is proven but expensive. One promises speed yet needs safeguards. The third keeps control in-house, at the cost of your team's capacity. The client needs an evidence-backed recommendation by 14:00."
**Question:** **Which deal can you defend after the launch — not just in today's pitch?**
**Exhibits:**
1. **SUPPLIER COMPARISON / 11:11**: price, implementation milestones, contingency and service ownership for three supplier routes.
2. **ACCESSIBILITY TEST / 11:17**: prototype interaction works for some users but a screen-reader and step-free check remain incomplete.
3. **DATA & DELIVERY TERMS / 11:21**: drafted opt-in/analytics, privacy obligations and warranty clauses; not all assumptions have been tested.
**Plans:**
- **CHOOSE PROVEN DELIVERY (£26k):** external review and tested accessible build; stronger confidence, slower/less flexible schedule.
- **NEGOTIATE A FAST ROUTE (£12k):** bounded milestones, verified owners and a contractual contingency; speed, but more exposure if assumptions fail.
- **BUILD THE ESSENTIALS (£18k):** put specialists on a smaller prototype using existing systems; greater control and operational progress, but workload risk.
**Public messages:** "Here are the milestones we can verify" / "We're targeting a faster delivery with checks at every step" / "We'll confirm performance before extending our commitment."
**Consequence:** whichever commitment is chosen becomes the boundary of what the next public teaser can truthfully claim. Optional £10k/£20k investments in controls/capacity/people remain possible, but must have coherent, tested impacts.

### 04 / 13:12 — THE SPOTLIGHT
**Hook:** "The client's marketing team publishes a scheduled teaser. It looks fantastic. Within an hour, people are asking where to sign up and whether all three sites will be open to everyone. The teaser shows a concept — but some details aren't approved. Excitement is good; a misleading promise isn't."
**Question:** **How do you keep the excitement without selling a fantasy?**
**Exhibits:**
1. **PUBLIC TEASER / 13:05**: attention and questions about opening dates, locations and access.
2. **APPROVAL CHECK / 13:09**: signed-off facts vs. draft concept, including which locations and features are conditional.
3. **COMMUNITY FEEDBACK / 13:11**: requests from a teacher, accessibility advocate and local partner; these are genuine requirements to address, not reputational villains.
**Plans:**
- **SHOW WHAT'S VERIFIED (£15k):** celebrate the idea while correcting assumptions and naming accountable milestones. Credibility, with more review work.
- **HOLD THE DETAIL (£9k):** acknowledge interest and publish details after checks. Protect accuracy; speculation can fill the silence.
- **INVITE PEOPLE IN (£19k):** run a targeted, moderated co-design session with user groups before making bold public commitments. Better inclusion/engagement; additional time and expense.
**Public messages:** "Here's what's confirmed" / "Help us shape the accessible pilot" / "We will announce dates when the venue checks are complete."
**Consequence:** the public now expects real evidence, and the next prototype dry run must test the features the team has discussed. No false accusation, dismissing accessibility, or fake public endorsement.

### 05 / 15:40 — THE REALITY CHECK
**Hook:** "The first demonstration is working — until the test venue loses reliable connectivity. It is a dry run, not a public launch. The onsite team can continue using an offline route, an expensive supported backup, or a reduced interactive programme. The client will arrive at 17:30 expecting a plan, not excuses."
**Question:** **What do you protect when the exciting plan meets a practical limit?**
**Exhibits:**
1. **DEMO RUN LOG / 15:16**: what succeeded/failed, in concrete terms; the issue is reproducible.
2. **BACKUP PROPOSAL / 15:27**: vendor support, a realistic activation time, documented costs and access restrictions.
3. **TEAM & ACCESS CHECK / 15:34**: an offline version is more reliable and accessible at some locations; team staffing/capacity is still finite.
**Plans:**
- **FUND THE BACKUP (£38k):** a vetted fallback supplier and controlled transition. Stronger reliability; significant cost and future close-out commitment.
- **REDESIGN WITH WHAT WORKS (£24k):** shift to a simpler offline-first experience and redeploy skilled colleagues. Fast robust fallback, but some headline features are postponed.
- **RESET THE SCOPE (£11k):** agree a narrower, provably deliverable pilot with the partner. Protects cash and staff; reduces immediate ambition.
**Public messages:** "These are the features we've tested" / "We're protecting the experience with a backup plan" / "We will confirm the next milestone after the demonstration."
**Consequence:** the team's *actual* cash, staff pressure, supplier commitments and client trust determine what the board can plausibly authorise. Optional £10k/£20k investment uses the same validated fund-accounting rules.

### 06 / 17:30 — THE FINAL CALL
**Hook:** "At 17:30, the client joins the video call. The board has your budget, the prototype results, the staff plan and every public promise you've made. You can't undo the day. You can choose an honest next step. Which plan would you defend six weeks from now?"
**Question:** **What should your company commit to next?**
**Exhibits:**
1. **THE DECISION LEDGER / 17:02**: authorised spending, remaining reserve, liabilities and key benefits — no phantom revenue.
2. **PARTNER SCORECARD / 17:18**: accessibility proof, operational readiness, honest communications, named ownership and cash feasibility.
3. **THE BOARD'S QUESTION / 17:29**: "What will you stop, delay or fund to make this actually work?"
**Plans:**
- **PHASE IT, PROVE IT (£28k):** funded milestones, independent checks, named owners and a realistic expansion sequence. Strong governance; commits reserve.
- **BACK THE BIGGER VISION (£18k + documented future exposure):** faster multi-site option with explicit dependencies. Upside for the client; capacity/schedule pressure.
- **DO ONE THING BRILLIANTLY (£7k):** demonstrably excellent one-site pilot first, with a truthful scope review. Cash and feasibility protected; opportunity may narrow.
**Public messages:** "Here's what we'll do — and what we won't" / "We're ready to accelerate these verified milestones" / "Our next handover will make progress inspectable."
**Consequence and finale:** display team-by-team **plan readiness**, believable stakeholder position, remaining reserve, unpaid obligations, programme viability and awards; do not pretend an unsigned contract or pilot has been officially awarded. Report should explain at least three causal moments from past choices.

## Adviser perspectives (six distinctive roles; no rigged answer bonus)
1. **LEGAL & ACCESSIBILITY** — inclusion, clear public claims, approval boundaries, contracts.
2. **OPERATIONS** — practical site readiness, delivery sequencing, contingencies.
3. **PARTNERSHIPS** — what the existing client and potential new partners actually need.
4. **FINANCE** — £260k reserve, authorised allocations, deferred promises, annual vs available revenue.
5. **TECH & DATA** — offline resilience, privacy, user evidence, data protection.
6. **PEOPLE & CULTURE** — realistic capacity, employee voice, collaboration, staff recovery.

Two advisers per chapter remain a **choice of information**, not magical score bonuses. Each contains a valid but incomplete viewpoint that matters more in some plans than others. A team can disagree without being judged as a "bad leader".

## Dynamic narrative and actual game-state checks
This is a **full rewrite**, not copy swaps. For each choice record: `planIndex`, `signalIndex`, optional allocation, cash spent, deferred liabilities, trust change and risk change; write one immediate response and one later callback that are *true of model state*. Do not silently carry over old `STORY_BRIDGES`, `SIGNAL_MOMENTS`, existing memo facts, rumours, restructuring references, incident reports, forensic access logs or old `fallout` prose.

Examples of bounded continuity:

- If it opted for **staff co-design**, bring forward the accessibility insight in the supplier/public chapters. If not, the same basic issue emerges from the venue check, without accusing players of discrimination.
- If it chose **fast supplier contracting**, show the actual unfunded risk/deferred cost. If a supplier is truly selected by the rules, mention that supplier, not a fictional alternative.
- If reserve/cash is below zero, never describe an action as fully financed; use a grounded "board would need new funding or smaller scope" warning.
- If trust has hit 100, avoid meaningless extra "trust hero" feedback; show actual risk/cash differences.
- The optional statement and funding must be acknowledged in outcome text rather than overwritten by the plan narrative.
- All six groups face the same public facts, but private team recaps differ according to their own choices. Do not leak adviser info into projector view.

## Game economics: required technical redesign, not relabelling
The existing `src/rules.js` economic model was balanced (with known limitations) for a *restructuring memo / client renewal* incident. This new opportunity changes the meaning of risk and the meaning of final "win". Retain the engine architecture where possible; **do not reuse original numeric deltas or champion thresholds without an explicit audit**.

Key concepts should be: **programme feasibility (operations), people capacity (workforce), claims/contract and accessibility compliance (legal), partner confidence (customer), reserve/commitment coverage (liquidity), combined trust**. Define the score as an authored fictional trade-off model, not a financial forecast or a validated employee assessment.

The `£260k` is an **organisation-wide 90-day reserve**; the `£240k` is annual existing client revenue (not cash available today); no new partner income is booked until the fiction explicitly supports it. Distinguish immediate authorised allocations from later payable liabilities with consistent UI text and ledger logic. Preserve no double charge, deterministic replay, valid score/risk bounding, privacy, 3- and 5-/7-player voting fairness, and all six-turn end-to-end tests.

Outcome wording: **"BOARD-READY"**, **"CONDITIONAL PLAN"**, **"REWORK REQUIRED"**, **"NOT VIABLE"** are candidate *internal fictional readiness* statuses. Do not merely rename `RENEWED` until thresholds and output semantics are reviewed. The highest-scoring team always receives a ranking, but "Champion" still requires announced defensible criteria. No client contract is described as signed unless a separately modelled, narratively justified outcome says so.

## Cross-segment adaptation without splitting the game
**Corporate event mode:** upbeat 45–60 minute facilitated story, visual team competition, concise choices, team awards, social debrief ("What did we disagree about?", "What surprised us?", "Would you choose differently?"). No leadership appraisal or diagnostic claims.

**Education mode:** same exact story and economics, plus optional teacher guide: budgets, operations, stakeholder decisions, ethical communication, accessibility, leadership and motivation. Debrief by observed decision trade-offs, not invented grades or accredited outcomes. Avoid legal/financial compliance advice presented as training. Suitable for business-related courses and non-specialists; suitability must be piloted with educators.

**Event facilitator:** exact same multiplayer Host/Join/Projector, no install/no account for joining, accessible short briefing, timed debate and ledger-driven reveal. Candidate 50–55 minute format remains a hypothesis.
