# BUSINESS CRISIS — GO-TO-MARKET V1 / UK + ROMANIA
**Date:** 5 November 2026  
**Owner-level planning, not validated sales performance.**  
**Source version:** six-episode `THE 09:17 INCIDENT`, hosted room, projector, 2–4 teams, read-only spectator and downloadable PDF verdict.  
**Non-negotiable product principle:** the game itself collects no email addresses and never sends results by email. Commercial prospecting, when lawful, is an entirely separate sales workflow.

## Executive answer
Sell **a facilitated live experience**, not a bare website or a dashboard. The hook: **"One leaked memo. Six impossible decisions. Who would you trust with your business?"** Target a 45–60-minute session including prebrief, approximately 20–30 minutes of choices and a guided debrief. The benefit we can credibly promise initially is an engaging competition and a shared discussion about decision-making under pressure. Do **not** claim measurable leadership development outcomes until we have evidence from real groups.

### Formats
- **Office / team-building:** one moderator operates the host console; a read-only link goes to the big screen; participants join on phones or laptops, ideally sitting at team tables.
- **Fully remote:** host shares the read-only projector in a Teams/Zoom/Meet call; separate breakout rooms for 2–4 teams; every participant joins the same room on their device, votes individually; host controls round transitions. Must test screen-sharing latency and audio before offering as fully supported.
- **Hybrid:** one host with some in-room teams and some remote breakout groups. Delay commercial commitment until full remote has proven stable.
- **Hostless / self-serve:** **defer**. It removes facilitation and changes the timing, tie-breaking, dispute, onboarding, and recovery model. Consider only after 10–20 successful facilitated sessions and clear buyer demand.

### Initial ideal customer profiles (ranked)
1. **UK HR / Learning & Development managers** at organisations with 100–2,000 staff, running manager away days, cross-functional team events and induction programmes.
2. **Corporate team-building agencies and freelance facilitators** able to resell a branded digital activity; potentially stronger long-term distribution than one-off cold outreach to individual businesses.
3. **Training providers and business educators** needing a repeatable decision-making simulation (student/event licensing is a separate pricing tier).
4. **Romanian corporate HR, training and event agencies** in București and Cluj-Napoca; differentiated by Romanian-language facilitation, not by fake localisation of UK-specific story facts.
5. **Consultancies and leadership coaches** seeking an experiential exercise; higher value, longer sales cycles.

## 90-day commercial experiment
**Days 1–14: product proof**
- Complete Mac Safari, iPhone Safari, Android Chrome, desktop Chrome/Edge and Firefox tests; 4:3 and 16:9 projector views; at least one 15–30-device realistic Wi-Fi test.
- Run 3 free/discounted facilitated groups: (1) office, (2) remote, (3) mixed-experience participants. Track where they stop, questions asked, incomplete ballots, host intervention burden and actual duration.
- Interview 5 potential buyers about purchase triggers and budget; record exact objections.

**Days 15–30: package and proof**
- Fix top five friction points from observations. Publish demo clip and product landing page. Prepare PDF sample and simple facilitator script.
- Propose 10 paid pilot conversations with L&D buyers/agencies. Do not guarantee a result before groups have tested it.

**Days 31–60: sell pilots**
- 30–50 well-researched, genuinely relevant organisations or agencies (not 500 scraped personal emails); 10 demo meetings as a target, not a promise.
- Test two offer framings: "corporate team challenge" vs "decision-making simulation". Record invitation-to-demo and demo-to-paid conversion.
- Aim for 3–5 paid pilots; if none convert, revisit fit and offer rather than spending on ads.

**Days 61–90: repeatability**
- Create facilitator checklist, issue log, recovery procedure, simple terms, device compatibility list, privacy documentation, buyer FAQ, sample verdict PDF and 2 real case studies.
- Test one repeat client and 2 agency distribution partnerships. Only then consider scalable self-serve licences.

## Commercial funnel and metrics
A qualified lead is a company/agency likely to run a 10–30-person event in the next 90 days **within tested capacity**. Track in a CRM:
`organisation / segment / publicly verifiable need / channel / company generic or lawfully sourced business contact / contact permission basis / last contact / next step / objections / demo / pilot / value / opt-out`.

Primary success metrics:
- **Operational:** crash-free sessions, WebSocket rejoin success, participant completion, median time-to-first-choice, 720p projector fit, download success on iOS.
- **Experience:** enjoyment, clarity, perceived challenge, willingness to recommend, learnings discussed (not inflated 'leadership improvement' claims).
- **Commercial:** reply rate, qualified-meeting rate, demo-to-pilot rate, profit per delivered session, repeat/agency revenue.
- Session economics: **net contribution = price - facilitator hours - travel - venue - platform costs - sales cost**. Never confuse gross bookings with profit.

## AI-assisted acquisition (human-governed)
AI can perform **company research, segmentation, qualification scoring, first-draft outreach, language localisation, creative variants, landing-page copy, transcript summaries, CRM deduplication, and post-demo analysis**. A human must review recipient fit, facts and messaging. Set a suppression list and audit trail before sending any external marketing.

**Pipeline:** company discovery → check sector/headcount/event-fit → verify legal marketing basis and public contact source → generate a *specific* reason to approach → human review → send through a separate commercial mail/CRM stack → honour opt-outs → one respectful follow-up if appropriate → stop on no-interest.

**UK marketing compliance:** Direct marketing to corporate subscribers generally does not require consent under PECR, but identity and a valid opt-out are mandatory; named contacts remain personal data under UK GDPR, and sole traders/individual subscribers are protected differently. Verify status and legitimate-interest assessment, and honour objections. Current ICO guidance:
- https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/advice-for-small-organisations/direct-marketing-and-data-protection/marketing-and-data-protection-in-detail/
- https://app-ico-public-prod-uksouth.azurewebsites.net/for-organisations/direct-marketing-and-privacy-and-electronic-communications/business-to-business-marketing/
For Romania/EU, review the applicable local ePrivacy/direct-marketing rules **before** an outbound campaign; UK corporate-email exceptions must not be assumed to apply unchanged. No unreviewed blasting, bought bulk lists, concealed identity or automatic emailing on a prospect's behalf.

## What to avoid
- Launching paid ads before a credible 60-second gameplay video and proven completion rate.
- Selling as a high-stakes compliance or accredited leadership assessment.
- Promising 30-player production reliability based only on unit/browser tests.
- Saying 'real-time branching AI narrative'—the current game has six fixed episodes and decision-dependent economics.
- Automating messages to every scraped profile, ignoring opt-outs or inventing client testimonials.
- Mixing lead generation contacts with private gameplay or the 24-hour temporary room store.
